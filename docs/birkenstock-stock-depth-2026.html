What gets flagged

  • It sold at least 10 pairs this year — below that the weekly pace means nothing — it was at least 20% below RRP, and one of these two applies:
  • DISCOUNT  It sold at 25% or more below RRP and was still shifting a pair a week or better. The discount was not what made it sell, so it was simply lost margin.
  • RAN OUT  It emptied before 20 August while still selling a pair a week. People still wanted it after the stock was gone, so the price was too low for how little we had.
  • Anything sold at under 20% off is not flagged, however fast it went. That is a normal trading discount, not a mistake.

Two problems repeat here. Arizona EVA — nine of the 22 flags are EVA colours. All are £50 RRP selling near £36 for £1–£4 a pair, and several shift three to five pairs a week. Bend Low trainers are the other: £135 RRP selling at £94, 110 pairs sold across the two colours, 114 pairs still sitting there.

Overall — too low and too quick, or stuck with it?

Both — but on different shoes. Too cheap on the fast sellers, and too deep on everything else. The second one is much the bigger number.

Sold too low, too quick ≈ £6,700

The flagged styles sold 719 pairs at 25–35% off RRP while still selling a pair a week or more. At a normal 15% off, those same pairs would have made about £6,700 more. Free money — but only a fifth of the year's £29,264.

Stuck with it 1,895 pairs · £65,805

1,849 of the 1,895 pairs left are in the 114 styles that still have a full size range — the ones that never sold fast enough to run down. That is £65,805 of cash sitting on the shelf: ten times the pricing loss on the left.

Pairs sold each month. *September is only to the 7th. Sandal season peaked in June and is over. Most of those 1,895 pairs will not move this year at any price — they wait for spring 2027. The exceptions are the Bend trainers and Zermatt slippers, which are autumn/winter stock and can still sell now.

What to do about it. Raising prices on the fast sellers is worth a few thousand a year and is worth doing. But the real money is in the buying. A third of the range is left over, and it is concentrated in a handful of styles: Bend Low Black (81 pairs left), Bend Low White (73), Arizona Dark Brown (64), Arizona Black (58). Those are the ones to order fewer of next season — not to discount harder now. The leftover stock is £131k at current prices against £66k of cost, and Birkenstock does not go out of fashion, so it is money tied up rather than money lost.

What counts as a sale. Every sale from 1 January to 7 September 2026, all channels, for products branded Birkenstock. A "style" is the whole product (the groupid), not the individual sizes.

What counts as stock. Sizes left is the number of different sizes with real, sellable stock on the shelf right now — free stock, not deleted, quantity above zero. The old stockvariants figure is not used because it is out of date.

What the money columns mean. Avg sold is the average price a pair actually went out at, after discounts — not the list price. Profit per unit is the average net profit on a pair. Pace is pairs per week between the style's first and last sale this year.

Sense check. Working from the brand recorded on each sale, rather than the product record as it stands today, gives 175 styles, 36 sold out and 25 with one size left — 34.9%. Same story. The small difference is a few styles that sold and have since been re-branded or removed.
